### DNS root zone rolls to KSK-2024 on 2026-10-11, and resolvers with stale trust anchors stop validating

- **Category:** Infrastructure
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.iana.org/dnssec/files), [ceremony schedule](https://www.iana.org/dnssec/ceremonies)
- **Blurb:** IANA rolls the DNS root zone to KSK-2024 on 2026-10-11, and resolvers with stale trust anchors stop validating.
- **Summary:** IANA's trust-anchor page schedules the rollover for 2026-10-11, when KSK-2024 begins signing the root zone and KSK-2017 stops, and states that validating resolvers must have updated trust anchors to continue validating the root zone. KSK-2024 has key tag 38696, was published in the root on 2025-01-11, and was expected to gain RFC 5011 trust from 2025-02-10, so resolvers that followed RFC 5011 automation through that window are already prepared. KSK-2017, key tag 20326, has signed since 2018-10-11 and is scheduled for revocation on 2027-01-11 and removal on 2027-03-22.
- **Why it matters:** A resolver with a manually pinned or stale trust anchor fails root validation outright one day from now rather than degrading quietly.
- **Follow-up:** Track validation failure reports after 2026-10-11 and the KSK-2017 revocation booked for 2027-01-11.

- **Follow-up:** The password detail rests on Politiken's review and no Danish authority has confirmed it, and the account of the initial leaked credential is an anonymous claim. The reported access window is left out here because the published figures did not agree and the primary returned no text for re-verification on this run. ### GhostAction injected malicious workflows into 346 repositories on 2026-10-08, exfiltrating CI secrets over plain HTTP

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://opensourcemalware.com/blog/ghostaction-attack-escalates), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50028187)
- **Blurb:** GhostAction committed malicious workflows to 346 repositories on 2026-10-08 and posted the harvested CI secrets over plain HTTP.
- **Summary:** The campaign commits workflow files to repository default branches, collects GitHub Actions secrets, and sends them to an attacker-controlled endpoint over unencrypted HTTP. The report counts 346 affected repositories with injections dated 2026-10-08, and states the workflows were still present on default branches on 2026-10-09. Socket lists a post with a matching headline on its blog index dated 2026-10-09, which did not resolve to an article URL on this run.
- **Why it matters:** Any repository touched by this wave must assume its Actions secrets are stolen, including credentials committed and later deleted, because the newer variant scans full git history and removing the workflow revokes nothing.
- **Follow-up:** Track further GhostAction waves and any platform-side response from GitHub.
