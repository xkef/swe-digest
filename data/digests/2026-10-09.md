## ML research

### Re-analysis of METR's time horizons finds a 3-minute to 30-minute jump is far easier than 30 minutes to 5 hours

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12466)
- **Blurb:** A re-analysis of METR's task data finds AI difficulty nearly flat from 2 to 30 minutes of measured human task time.
- **Summary:** The paper recomputes 228 tasks across 26 models using splines and item-response theory. The resulting conversion from human task time to AI difficulty is nearly flat between 2 and 30 minutes, then steepens.
- **Why it matters:** Equal multipliers on the widely cited time-horizon plot do not represent equal capability gains, so a doubling rate read off that curve cannot be extrapolated past the half-hour mark.

### fast-jwt accepted expired tokens from its verifier cache when the JWT carried no iat claim

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-x937-hj6v-793p)
- **Blurb:** fast-jwt up to 6.3.3 keeps serving expired tokens from its cache for the full TTL when the JWT carries no iat claim.
- **Summary:** On fast-jwt versions up to 6.3.3 with caching enabled, a token carrying `exp` but no `iat` stays accepted from the verifier cache for the full `cacheTTL`, which defaults to 10 minutes. The flaw is tracked as CVE-2026-107719 and fixed in 6.3.4.
- **Why it matters:** With caching enabled, a token holding exp but no iat stays accepted for the full cacheTTL, 10 minutes by default, after it expires, so expiry stops bounding a stolen bearer token on versions up to 6.3.3.
