### AWS says data held only in its Bahrain region cannot be restored

- **Category:** Outage
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [DatacenterDynamics report](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/aws-unable-to-restore-access-to-data-centers-hit-by-iran-strikes/), [HN submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49766505)
- **Blurb:** AWS states that data held only in Bahrain me-south-1 or the mec1-az2 zone of UAE me-central-1 cannot be restored.
- **Summary:** DatacenterDynamics, in a report dated 2026-09-16, says AWS provided undated status updates stating that resources and data held exclusively in me-south-1, or exclusively in the mec1-az2 availability zone of me-central-1, cannot be restored. The report attributes the loss to physical damage: the Bahrain and UAE facilities were struck by Iranian drones during the US-Israel-Iran conflict, with a second attack on Bahrain claimed by the IRGC in July. The Bahrain statement is the one saying the damage exceeded what its regional and multi-AZ services are designed to withstand, while for the UAE, AWS says it continues to work on recovering regional resources and the mec1-az1 and mec1-az3 zones. AWS says it is replacing the affected infrastructure, will update on restoration in the coming months, and will share its plans for Bahrain in early 2027. An Amazon spokesperson told DatacenterDynamics that reading the updates as a decision not to reopen the sites contradicts them, then declined to say whether the sites reopen with new hardware or as new facilities. The report predates this page and the Hacker News thread resurfaced on 2026-09-19.
- **Why it matters:** Any recovery plan that treats an AWS region as the durability boundary has to be re-derived from cross-region copies, because AWS now states in-region data is gone rather than delayed.
- **Follow-up:** Track the restoration update AWS says is coming and the Bahrain plans promised for early 2027.

### Hash audit reports key-free multicollisions in 13 non-cryptographic hashes

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [Ahle post](https://thomasahle.com/blog/adversarial-examples-for-hashes/), [HN submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49775383)
- **Blurb:** An audit reports key-independent multicollisions for 13 non-cryptographic hashes, including CityHash64 and MurmurHash3.
- **Summary:** Thomas Ahle published an audit on his own site reporting arbitrarily many inputs that collide under every seed, constructed without knowing the key, for gxhash, MUM, mir, mx3, fasthash, pengyhash, MuseAir v0.3, a5hash-128, nmhash32x, Abseil Hash, CityHash64, FarmHash64 and MurmurHash3. The post states he disclosed upstream before publishing, links maintainer replies for xxHash, komihash, MuseAir and foldhash, and reports a maintainer consensus that only true multicollision attacks are worth fixing. Two limits are stated in the post itself and a reader would not infer either. The per-hash appendix was produced with model assistance, the author warns it contains errors and says he trusts only the concrete examples he found and measured, so the claims that carry weight are the named witnesses and the machine-checked Lean bounds. The bounds are on equality of the full 64-bit output, and the post is explicit that a full-output bound does not automatically carry to a bucket index, so the practical hash-table claim is narrower than the headline. Version scope is thin: the post names a version for MuseAir v0.3 and a5hash-128 only, so for the other eleven, including CityHash64, FarmHash64, MurmurHash3 and Abseil Hash, which versions the witnesses were constructed against is not stated.
- **Why it matters:** The named hashes sit under hash tables that take attacker-controlled keys, and a collision family built without knowledge of the seed leaves seed randomization as a defence that does not hold.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether any of the 13 named hashes ships a fix or a documented adversarial-input caveat.

### PyPy 8.0.0 adds Python 3.12 in beta and lays the groundwork for abi3 wheels

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [PyPy release post](https://pypy.org/posts/2026/09/pypy-v800-release.html), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49770701)
- **Blurb:** PyPy 8.0.0 ships Python 3.12 at beta quality and aligns its PyObject layout so cp312-abi3 wheels can later install.
- **Summary:** The release dated 2026-09-19 adds Python 3.12 support at beta quality alongside 2.7 and 3.11, raises the Linux tarball floor to glibc 2.28 through manylinux_2_28, which is why the major version moved, drops the internal HPy backend behind a build option, and marks 3.11 as the last release of that line barring security fixes. For packaging, PyPy now hides its `ob_pypy_link` extension field behind a prefix so `PyObject` matches CPython's layout under `Py_LIMITED_API=0x030C0000`, and it no longer mangles limited-API exports, so `PyTuple_New` is exported under its own name instead of `PyPyTupleNew`. The goal is installing `cp312-abi3` wheels built for CPython, and it is not usable yet, because the import machinery must still accept `abi3.so` objects and pip and uv must still treat those wheels as candidates.
- **Why it matters:** Reusing CPython abi3 wheels would remove the main packaging reason extension-heavy projects skip PyPy, once the import and installer changes land.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether PyPy import machinery, pip and uv accept `cp312-abi3` wheels on PyPy.

### Brood War Bench reports no model played above beginner level across 171 matches

- **Category:** AI
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [benchmark report](https://bw.swerdlow.dev/report), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49766966)
- **Blurb:** Brood War Bench reports no model configuration played above beginner level across 171 StarCraft matches.
- **Summary:** Ben Swerdlow self-published a round-robin benchmark of 171 matches and states that no configuration played beyond beginner level, and that a human beginner running a photon rush would win every game. On the leaderboard Codex Astra at xhigh effort went 18-0, while the three Grok configurations and Claude Haiku each won at most two games. The per-game notes record a multi-agent failure: Codex spawned separate subagents for economy, army production and army control, the subagents did not communicate, and the army subagent sent each finished unit to attack alone rather than massing. The author also reports that models treating a real-time game as turn-based lost while thinking, and says that may explain why some lower-effort settings performed better than higher-effort ones. Method is disclosed, including the round-robin matrix run in parallel on Freestyle VMs with engine data and both harness logs saved per match, and costs are stated as token-based estimates for Codex and Sonnet.
- **Why it matters:** A vendor-independent harness reporting that every configuration failed is a usable data point on long-horizon real-time control, and the subagent coordination failure is worth reading before building a similar harness.

### Paper shows a model can fingerprint its own inference engine and exploit it

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20614), [HN submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49772432)
- **Blurb:** A preprint shows a model can identify the inference engine running it and exploit it using only its own output tokens.
- **Summary:** The preprint was submitted 2026-09-17 by Sarah Radway, Andrew Cheng, Vijay Janapa Reddi and James Mickens, and is not peer reviewed. It gives fingerprints for five popular engines, names vLLM and SGLang, shows realistic agentic harnesses in which a model identifies the local engine, and describes a proof-of-concept exploit chain from the compromised engine to the bare metal that needs only the model's own output tokens and no maliciously crafted input. The preprint does not specify which engine versions are affected. The authors cite the recent frontier-model sandbox escapes at OpenAI and Anthropic as motivation, and close with engine changes that would make fingerprinting harder.
- **Why it matters:** The inference engine is the target rather than the components around it, so sandboxing the network proxy and the code-execution environment does not close the path for anyone self-hosting an engine behind an agent harness.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether vLLM or SGLang adopt the proposed anti-fingerprinting changes.

### Typst 0.15 emits MathML from HTML export and adds multi-document bundles

- **Category:** Dev tools
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [LWN review](https://lwn.net/Articles/1092993/), [HN submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49765941)
- **Blurb:** Typst 0.15 renders mathematics as MathML in HTML export and builds linked HTML and PDF bundles from one source.
- **Summary:** HTML export now renders mathematics as MathML, which current browsers interpret without MathJax and without falling back to images, and LWN reports the HTML and PDF renderings of the same source as essentially identical. The new bundle output builds a linked set of HTML and PDF files from one source with working links between them, which covers publishing a slide deck and a preprint together or generating a small site. The release also adds variable-font axis control, multiple bibliographies with per-section numbering, and targeting several PDF standards at once, where the compiler refuses incompatible pairs such as PDF 1.7 with PDF/A-1b and enforces requirements such as the document title that PDF/UA-1 needs. Version 0.15 shipped in June 2026 and the LWN review is dated 2026-09-09, so both predate this page. Two governance facts sit with the tool: the project rejects any contribution generated by an LLM, and its maintainers state Typst is a company as well as an open-source project and that company needs inform project direction.
- **Why it matters:** Mathematics survives HTML export without a JavaScript renderer, while Typst remains pre-1.0 and LWN notes journals requiring source submissions still do not accept it.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether HTML export and bundle output leave experimental status behind the `--features` flag.

## Apple platforms

### Lemire decomposes the 50 percent single-core gain from Apple M2 to M5

- **Category:** Apple
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [Lemire blog post](https://lemire.me/blog/2026/09/19/how-did-apple-silicon-get-50-faster-in-three-years/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49770894)
- **Blurb:** Lemire attributes most of the 52 percent M2 to M5 single-core gain to clock, with vector width unchanged at 128 bits.
- **Summary:** Daniel Lemire reports Geekbench 6 single-core moving from 2,401 to 3,642 and multi-core from 9,814 to 17,955 across the base M2, M3, M4 and M5, which he states as 52 percent and 83 percent. P-core peak clock rose from 3.5 to 4.6 GHz, about 30 percent, which he says explains the bulk of the single-core figure, while decode width went from 8 to 10 instructions per cycle, bandwidth from 100 to 154 GB/s, and the base part gained two efficiency cores at the M4. Apple stayed at four 128-bit execution units from the M2 through the M5, against four 512-bit units on Zen 5, and what is new instead is the 512-bit SME matrix unit on the M4 and M5. He states he could not find a transistor count for the M5, and this is a third-party analysis of published benchmark numbers rather than an Apple disclosure.
- **Why it matters:** Throughput assumptions tuned to AVX-512 width do not carry over to Apple silicon, so vector-heavy code targets the SME matrix unit rather than wider general vectors.

### Dragonfly 2.0 adds Valkey 9 RDB loading and makes connection memory accounting constant time

- **Category:** Infrastructure
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [v2.0.0 release notes](https://github.com/dragonflydb/dragonfly/releases/tag/v2.0.0), [Phoronix report](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Dragonfly-2.0-Released), [HN submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49774336)
- **Blurb:** Dragonfly 2.0 loads Valkey 9 RDB files, makes connection memory introspection O(1), and rejects oversized RESTORE payloads.
- **Summary:** The release notes name operational changes rather than a version bump. Dragonfly 2.0 was published 2026-09-16, so it predates this page. It loads RDB files written by Valkey 9, connection memory introspection drops from O(n) to O(1), and the default maximum client input buffer halves from 64 KiB to 32 KiB. RDB serializer buffers are capped at 4 MiB after flushing, where a 512 MiB object previously left 512 MiB allocated. RESTORE now rejects payloads whose declared lengths exceed the remaining input, which the notes say prevents remote out-of-memory crashes.
- **Why it matters:** The serializer buffer cap and the RESTORE length check close two paths by which one large or malformed payload drives a Dragonfly node out of memory.

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed

- **Summary:** The measurements cover the Adreno X2 GPU in Qualcomm's Snapdragon X2 Elite.
