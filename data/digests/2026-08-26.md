### DuckLabs will join AWS in early September, and DuckDB stays MIT licensed under the nonprofit DuckDB Foundation

- **Category:** Infrastructure
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://ducklabs.com/news/2026/08/26/ducklabs-to-join-aws), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49448321)
- **Blurb:** The DuckDB company joins AWS in early September while the nonprofit Foundation keeps the MIT-licensed projects and the IP.
- **Summary:** DuckLabs published an announcement dated 2026-08-26, signed by Mark Raasveldt and Hannes Muehleisen, stating that the company will join AWS with the move expected effective in early September. The post states that DuckDB, DuckLake, Quack, and the other open-source components stay free and MIT licensed, that the nonprofit DuckDB Foundation continues to hold the IP and steward the projects, and that the team stays together in Amsterdam. Two commitments are stated and not yet delivered: a technical advisory board inside the Foundation, and opening the extension stack so extensions signed by other developers and organizations can run. The post quotes Andy Warfield of AWS, Peter Boncz for CWI on the Foundation board, Torsten Grust of Tuebingen, Jordan Tigani of MotherDuck, and George Fraser of Fivetran. No terms are disclosed, and this run read no AWS-side page, so the block rests on the DuckLabs announcement alone. The Hacker News title reads `AWS Acquires DuckDB`, which is not what the announcement states: the company is acquired and the projects stay with the Foundation.
- **Why it matters:** DuckDB reports more than one million downloads a day and sits inside a large number of data pipelines, so the MIT license and the Foundation, rather than founder independence, are what a downstream user now relies on.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether the Foundation's technical advisory board and the signed-extension stack arrive, and whether AWS publishes its own statement or any terms.

### Researcher signs forged C2PA images on a fully patched Pixel, and Google closes the report as Won't fix (infeasible)

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.da.vidbuchanan.co.uk/blog/android-c2pa.html), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49439499)
- **Blurb:** A researcher signed forged C2PA images on a fully patched Pixel, and Google closed the report as Won't fix.
- **Summary:** David Buchanan published first-hand research signing forged C2PA images and video on a fully patched Pixel device, with a proof of concept. The post's thesis is that C2PA on Android cannot be realistically patched, because low-cost hardware fault injection yields valid signatures regardless of patch level, and the published demo image and video were signed through the hardware attack rather than the software path. It names CVE-2026-43499 as a one-click root path that still works at current Pixel patch levels with the bootloader locked, and states that Meta patched that CVE on Quest earlier this month while Google has shipped no Pixel patch. Google closed the report as Won't fix (infeasible) and paid a 7,500 dollar bounty for an attack class its bug bounty formally excludes. The post states that every Android C2PA camera app resting on Key Attestation or Play Integrity breaks the same way, so the result is not Pixel-specific. One counterexample is in the post: the author's PTE bit-flip step stopped working on a Samsung A07 after an update enabled Samsung's RKP mitigation, whose EL2 hypervisor blocks the overwrite, and he states he has untested plans to work around it. The post also states that a separate private key disclosure was reported and patched within a day while most C2PA verifiers do not check revocation.
- **Why it matters:** C2PA on Android rests on Key Attestation and Play Integrity, a rooted-but-locked device defeats both, and the hardware path defeats them at any patch level, so the signature is not evidence that a camera captured the image.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether Google revises the hardware attestation basis for C2PA on Android, or the bug bounty scope for this attack class.
