## Top stories

### Attackers hijack three ccTLDs and obtain counterfeit TLS certificates for Google and other large services

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [report](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/hackers-obtain-counterfeit-tls-certificates-for-google-and-other-large-services/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49988230)
- **Blurb:** Google reports attackers hijacked the .gh, .sl and .as registries and passed domain validation to mint certificates for Google.
- **Summary:** Google reported on 2026-10-06 that attackers compromised the .gh, .sl and .as country code top-level domains, modified authoritative DNS records and nameserver delegations for selected domains, and passed automated domain control validation to obtain certificates for several Google domains and for other leading global brands and online services. No affected domain owner's own infrastructure was compromised and the certificate authorities followed all requirements, so the failure sits in the registry and validation path rather than in any one operator. Google updated Chrome to block the certificates it identified and had those for Google properties revoked, while stating it cannot guarantee it found every affected domain and that Chrome interventions do not protect non-Chrome users.
- **Why it matters:** The actions available to a domain owner are monitoring certificate transparency logs for unexpected issuance and publishing restrictive CAA records to stop reuse of cached validation data.
- **Follow-up:** Watch for registry-level remediation at the three ccTLDs and for a response on cached validation reuse.

### OpenSSH 10.6 disables the LZ77 dictionary coder to mitigate a cross-channel plaintext recovery attack

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.openssh.org/releasenotes.html), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07709), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49983791)
- **Blurb:** OpenSSH 10.6 disables the LZ77 dictionary coder to mitigate a cross-channel plaintext recovery attack on shared compression.
- **Summary:** OpenSSH 10.6 was released on 2026-10-06 with several security fixes, headlined by disabling the LZ77 dictionary coder to mitigate a chosen-plaintext attack that recovers secrets from one channel by interacting with the SSH session's shared compression dictionary through another channel, described in a preprint by Fabian Baeumer and Marcus Brinkmann. The release notes state the documentation already recommended against enabling Compression for sessions carrying both trusted and untrusted traffic, so the mitigated attack requires a non-default option, and the Compression option will be measurably less effective after the change, with application-level compression recommended instead. Other fixes restrict backslash and dollar characters in command-line usernames to block shell injection through ProxyCommand and Match exec, tighten sftp path validation against writes outside the target directory, and correct GSSAPI credential handling.
- **Why it matters:** The release notes state that the team is shipping more frequently because security bugs found by AI tooling are being independently rediscovered by other researchers, which the maintainers read as evidence that non-reporting adversaries can find them too.

### OpenAI opens a Decisions API in public beta

- **Category:** AI
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49984025)
- **Blurb:** OpenAI opened a Decisions API in public beta that returns calibrated probabilities and typed choices instead of generated text.
- **Summary:** The endpoint returns typed answers rather than generated text, in three question forms: predicate returns a probability from 0 to 1, choice selects from supplied values, and score returns a probability-weighted average over ordered levels. OpenAI states it is about 10 times faster than the Responses API and that gpt-6-luna is the only model currently available. It is a public beta with general availability stated as weeks away, so it is not yet a stable dependency.
- **Why it matters:** This changes the cost of classification, routing and prioritization steps that today burn a full generation call, and the calibrated probability is the part structured outputs does not give.

### AWS releases Strands Decider 2B, an open-source decision model

- **Category:** AI
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://strandsagents.com/blog/introducing-strands-decider/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49987076)
- **Blurb:** AWS released Strands Decider 2B on 2026-10-01 with weights and training data, reporting median decision latency near 115ms on an RTX 3090.
- **Summary:** Strands Decider 2B is a 2 billion parameter decision model announced on 2026-10-01 and released with weights on Hugging Face plus the training data and scripts, which is a fuller release than most model drops, and it reaches this digest through a Hacker News thread that surfaced today rather than as a release of the day. The architecture takes a Qwen3.5-2B torso, removes the language modelling head and replaces it with a roughly one million parameter pointer head that scores option positions against an answer position, with the torso fine-tuned through a rank-16 LoRA adapter. AWS reports a median decision latency around 115ms on an RTX 3090 and about 153ms for small tasks on an M3 MacBook, plus placement 3rd of 33 in the 2B class on JevBench accuracy and Brier calibration.
- **Why it matters:** Alongside the OpenAI Decisions API the same week, this marks decision models as a distinct class aimed at the routing, tool-gating and guardrail decisions where a full LLM call is too slow and too expensive to sit in the path.

## ML research

### Preprint claims truly subquadratic 3SUM and truly subcubic APSP, refuting both hypotheses

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06783), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49977437)
- **Blurb:** A preprint claims deterministic 3SUM in O(n^1.9992) and APSP in O(n^2.9995), refuting the hardness hypotheses built on both.
- **Summary:** Josh Alman and Virginia Vassilevska Williams posted a 76 page preprint on 2026-10-05 giving deterministic 3SUM in O(n^1.9992) and directed APSP with polynomially bounded integer weights in O(n^2.9995), the first polynomial improvements over the textbook algorithms. If correct the paper refutes the 3SUM and APSP hypotheses, their real-valued versions, the Exact Triangle hypothesis, the Zero-Weight k-Clique hypotheses and three rectangular hinted Online Matrix-Vector conjectures. Everything follows from one new algorithm for thin matrix products, built by modifying a variant of Coppersmith's rectangular matrix multiplication.
- **Why it matters:** Those hypotheses are the assumed hardness underneath a large body of fine-grained complexity lower bounds, so a correct result invalidates the conditional part of many published hardness claims, while the speedups are far too small to change any implementation.
- **Follow-up:** Watch for peer review or a counterexample to the thin matrix product algorithm.

## Agentic coding

### Armin Ronacher on what Codemode is and where it runs

- **Category:** Agentic coding
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/10/6/codemode/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49978333)
- **Blurb:** Armin Ronacher separates the trusted harness from the sandboxed shell and places Codemode scripting on the harness side.
- **Summary:** The useful distinction in the post is between the harness, which is trusted and holds the model APIs, and the execution environment, which runs bash under sandboxing. Codemode lets the model script tool calls on the harness side, in Pi's case inside QuickJS on a WASM runtime with no network, no filesystem, no timers and limited RAM. That buys three things bash cannot: structured access to larger tool output without pushing it through context, concurrency and basic workflows expressed directly, and state stashed into the transcript for later calls to read back.
- **Why it matters:** Ronacher names the failure modes, including Codemode inside an MCP server that already does Codemode, which forces double JSON escaping and prevents the inner code from calling outer tools, and his concrete asks of MCP servers are structured content via outputSchema, consistent result shapes across batch sizes, large binary data support, and composable tool search across servers.

### Informal benchmark finds Python 3.15 roughly flat, with the JIT ahead of the standard interpreter

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://blog.miguelgrinberg.com/post/how-fast-is-python-3-15), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49984652)
- **Blurb:** An informal benchmark puts CPython 3.15 near flat against 3.14, with the JIT beating the standard interpreter for the first time.
- **Summary:** Miguel Grinberg ran recursion-heavy and loop-heavy microbenchmarks across CPython 3.10 to 3.15, plus JIT and free-threading builds, PyPy, Node and Rust. The standard 3.15 interpreter is only marginally faster than 3.14 and slightly slower on one multi-threaded test, so there is no performance reason to rush an upgrade. The new result is the JIT, which for the first time beats the standard interpreter of the same version by 1.20x to 1.28x, having previously trailed it, while free-threading 3.15 runs about 4.5 times faster than the standard interpreter on the multi-threaded recursion test and still trails it on single-threaded work.
- **Why it matters:** The author labels the benchmark informal, describes the two programs and the averaging method, notes he tested 3.15.0rc3 rather than the final release, separates his upgrade opinion from the measurements, and recommends against the still-experimental JIT in production.

## Engineering posts

### matklad argues microbenchmarks should be tuned to about 300ms

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://matklad.github.io/2026/10/05/benchmark-milliseconds.html), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49967427)
- **Blurb:** matklad argues microbenchmarks should be sized to about 300ms, long enough to drown fixed costs and short enough to rerun ten times.
- **Summary:** The rule is to adjust input size until a microbenchmark takes roughly 300ms. The reasoning is specific: milliseconds in the 1 to 999 range are integers that scan visually without unit switching, anything under about 10ms risks being dominated by fixed costs such as interpreter startup, hundreds of milliseconds is long enough to make one-off overheads irrelevant without more fragile correction techniques, and anything over a second makes running the benchmark ten times to eyeball variance too slow.
- **Why it matters:** The underlying claim is that benchmarking exists to give the author enough intuition to make a correct decision rather than to produce a precise performance measurement, which is directly applicable to anyone writing a benchmark harness.
