### OpenAI says an internal model proved finite-time blowup for Navier-Stokes, and mathematicians dispute the provenance

- **Category:** AI
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-claims-blockbuster-math-breakthrough-amid-swirl-of-controversy/), [statement](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262)
- **Blurb:** OpenAI says an internal model produced a Lean-certified Navier-Stokes blowup proof, and Tristan Buckmaster disputes its provenance.
- **Summary:** Scientific American reported on 2026-09-08 that OpenAI states an internal model, which OpenAI has not named, produced a proof of finite-time blowup for the Navier-Stokes equations certified in Lean, and the article characterizes that claim as the second solution ever of one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems. The same article reports Tristan Buckmaster alleging the result draws on work he and Levent Alpoge, a mathematician employed by Anthropic, had not yet released. OpenAI mathematician Sebastien Bubeck denied that at a press briefing, said the Navier-Stokes solution followed a method similar to the two mathematicians' while the Euler result did not, and said the proof was developed over the weekend, which the article notes is after the point at which Buckmaster claims news of his result had reached OpenAI.
- **Why it matters:** OpenAI states the proof was certified in Lean, which makes the claim machine-checkable rather than prose, and the unresolved question is whether the lab's result was independent of work it had already learned about.
- **Follow-up:** Track the release of the Lean artifacts and any independent account of the timeline.

### Alpoge and Buckmaster publish finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for 3D Euler, Boussinesq and incompressible porous media

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://mastodon.social/@tristanbuckmaster/117233413705701198), [assessment](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117233527638291447), [repository](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/fluid_lean), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605915)
- **Blurb:** Alpoge and Buckmaster released Lean-formalized finite-time blowup results with smooth forcing for 3D Euler and two related systems.
- **Summary:** Tristan Buckmaster announced on his own account that he and Levent Alpoge proved finite-time blowup with a smooth forcing term for 3D incompressible Euler, the Boussinesq system, and incompressible porous media, which is not the unforced Navier-Stokes Millennium problem. The arguments are formalized in Lean, and the repository carries `affinecore`, `boussinesq-blowup` and `euler-blowup` directories. Terence Tao posted an assessment the same day naming the Cordoba and Martinez-Zoroa approach, confirming the Lean formalization, stating the arguments contain significant AI input that the authors spent recent weeks reworking into an acceptable form, and stating the authors released far earlier than planned because of external events.
- **Why it matters:** The arguments are formalized in Lean and the code is public, so the AI contribution sits behind a proof checker rather than behind a claim, which is the standard the weaker AI-proof announcements of the past year did not meet.

### A researcher factored two 512-bit certificate authority root keys shipped in Netscape 4.51

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://mcpherrin.ca/2026/09/07/rsa.html), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49604637)
- **Blurb:** A researcher factored both 512-bit E-Certify root keys from Netscape 4.51 in 32 and 29 hours on one desktop Ryzen.
- **Summary:** A researcher ran CADO-NFS on a single Ryzen 9 5950X desktop to factor two 512-bit RSA certificate authority root keys that shipped in the Netscape 4.51 trust store. The two E-Certify root private keys were recovered in 32 hours and 29 hours. There is no live exposure: Netscape removed the roots in 2002, they expired on 2003-10-16, and using the recovered keys requires running Netscape 4.51 with the clock set back.
- **Why it matters:** It puts a measured single-machine cost on RSA key sizes that once anchored browser trust stores, without touching any current trust store.
