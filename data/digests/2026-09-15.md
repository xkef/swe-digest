### Microsoft ships an out-of-band update after its September patch destabilized Remote Desktop Services and broke Plan9 host shares for WSL

- **Category:** Infrastructure
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/release-health/status-windows-11-24h2), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49699297)
- **Blurb:** Microsoft released out-of-band update KB5129195 on 2026-09-14 to fix Remote Desktop Services instability and broken WSL host shares.
- **Summary:** Microsoft's Windows release health page lists three regressions from the 2026-09-08 security update KB5124008, and out-of-band cumulative update KB5129195, released 2026-09-14, marks Remote Desktop Services instability and the Plan9 host folder share failure as resolved. Microsoft names WSL and Claude Cowork as affected by the Plan9 break, and states that administrators who applied the Group Policy mitigation for that issue must re-enable the policy before installing the update. The USB Audio Class 1.0 entry is still marked Mitigated, and Microsoft states the fix addresses the 8-channel and 3D audio symptoms only.
- **Why it matters:** The Remote Desktop Services fix spans Windows Server 2012 through 2025 and the USB audio regression is only partly resolved, so KB5129195 is scheduled work rather than an optional catch-up.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether Microsoft moves the USB Audio Class 1.0 entry from Mitigated to Resolved.

### Sakana AI reports training 1000-layer networks without backpropagation using layer-local predictive coding

- **Category:** ML research
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [primary](https://pub.sakana.ai/pc-alm/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31022), [code](https://github.com/SakanaAI/pc-alm), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49701182)
- **Blurb:** Sakana AI reports training 1000-layer networks with layer-local predictive coding and no backpropagation pass.
- **Summary:** Sakana AI describes PC-ALM, a layer-local predictive coding method it reports trains networks of 1000 layers without a backpropagation pass, which the authors claim is the first layer-local method to reach that depth. The 1000-layer result is reported on MNIST only, at width 32, in residual MLPs, while CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet appear as ResNet-18 comparisons against standard predictive coding at ordinary depth. The write-up carries only a September 2026 date and cites arXiv 2605.31022, and no third party has reproduced the numbers.
- **Why it matters:** Every layer in PC-ALM communicates only with its neighbours, so the result bears on distributed and neuromorphic training hardware where backpropagation's global forward-then-backward ordering is the expensive constraint.
- **Follow-up:** Track an independent reproduction of the 1000-layer result outside the authors' benchmark set.

### An ICML paper reports that discounting correlated LLM judges beats weighted majority vote by 9 to 14 percent

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.amazon.science/blog/when-llm-judges-agree-should-we-believe-them), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49699590)
- **Blurb:** Amazon researchers report a judge-aggregation method that beats weighted majority vote by 9 to 14 percent on existing logs.
- **Summary:** An Amazon Science post dated 2026-08-26 describes an ICML 2026 paper on aggregating the votes of multiple LLM judges. The method discounts judges whose errors are correlated, and the authors report a 9 to 14 percent improvement over weighted majority vote. The correlation estimate is derived from evaluation logs rather than from new labelled data.
- **Why it matters:** Teams that add judges to a panel to reduce noise may be adding correlated votes instead, and the method measures that from evaluation logs those teams already collect.

### Quay.io pushes and pulls degraded for about fourteen hours while Red Hat updated three affected database tables

- **Category:** Outage
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://status.redhat.com/incidents/0g8rsg3tbjpz), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49705394)
- **Blurb:** Red Hat's Quay.io ran about fourteen hours degraded on 2026-09-14, with only pulls served from 15:35 UTC until recovery.
- **Summary:** Red Hat's status page opens the incident at 2026-09-14 13:34 UTC as degraded performance on the Quay.io push and pull API, and the 14:02 and 14:04 UTC updates state Red Hat identified the cause of the image pull failures. Read-only mode, described as only pulls being served, began at 15:35 UTC and ran until the incident closed at 04:05 UTC. Red Hat states the recovery required updating three affected database tables, and Clair vulnerability scanning stayed disabled after push and pull recovered.
- **Why it matters:** A registry that answers pulls but refuses pushes keeps deployments running while release pipelines stall, so this class of incident surfaces in build jobs well before anything user-facing breaks.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether Red Hat restores Clair scanning and publishes a root cause.

## Linux and kernel

### An eBPF security agent caches policy lookups per inode and cuts benchmark kernel cycles from 28 billion to 3.03 billion

- **Category:** Linux/Kernel
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://nathannaveen.dev/posts/dropping-ebpf-cpu-cost-by-90/), [code](https://github.com/bomfather/agent), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49697477)
- **Blurb:** Per-inode caching of policy lookups cut the agent's benchmark kernel cycles by about 90 percent, from 28 to 3.03 billion.
- **Summary:** A post dated 2026-09-11 describes caching policy lookups per inode inside an eBPF security agent. The benchmark opened the same file 200,000 times, and the author reports kernel cycles falling from 28 billion to 3.03 billion. Hardlinks take a fallback path outside the cache, which the author describes as choosing accuracy over cache coverage.
- **Why it matters:** The cache lives inside the agent, so an operator collects the reported reduction from an upgrade alone without rewriting a single policy.
