## Top stories

### Real-SWE reports every frontier model below 40 percent on tasks from private enterprise codebases

- **Category:** Agentic coding
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [benchmark](https://withspecific.com/benchmarks/real-swe), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49676820)
- **Blurb:** Real-SWE scored every frontier model below 40 percent on software tasks drawn from private enterprise codebases.
- **Summary:** Specific Labs published Real-SWE, a benchmark it built and licensed itself, with resolution rates computed over ten analyzed sample tasks at eight rollouts per model, so 80 runs per model. Method, harness pairing, cost and a failure taxonomy are disclosed. The tasks sit on private codebases and cannot be reproduced by third parties.
- **Why it matters:** The published rates bound what coding agents finish unattended on real production work, and the failure taxonomy names missed requirements and unverified assumptions as the dominant modes.

### Rust 1.99 will stabilize the never type and make Infallible an alias for it

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [LWN](https://lwn.net/SubscriberLink/1091015/d9e48318ed242b41/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49625056)
- **Blurb:** Rust 1.99 will stabilize the never type and alias Infallible to it, with crater finding 3,300 affected crates.
- **Summary:** The upcoming release stabilizes the never type and redefines Infallible as an alias for it. The accompanying fallback change breaks calls to generic functions that lack sufficient type information. Crater found 3,300 crates affected, seven of them fully broken, and backports fixed 1,553 ahead of the release.
- **Why it matters:** A crate that relied on the old fallback needs an explicit type annotation to keep compiling, so the fix sits with library maintainers before the release rather than with users at upgrade time.

### Apple M3 Neural Engine DMA erratum throttles weight streaming at exact 1 MiB transfer sizes

- **Category:** Apple
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [writeup](https://eiln.github.io/posts/ane-dma.html), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49636479)
- **Blurb:** An Apple M3 Neural Engine DMA erratum throttles weight streaming at exactly 1 MiB transfer sizes, cutting decode rate.
- **Summary:** Transfers sized at exactly 1 MiB hit a DMA erratum that throttles weight streaming into the M3 Neural Engine. Splitting transfers off the 1 MiB boundary raised Llama 3.2 1B decode throughput from 10.0 to 24.3 tokens per second. This is a separate post from the ANE register-interface writeup carried on 2026-09-12.
- **Why it matters:** The remedy is a transfer-sizing change in the compiler, so shipped M3 hardware recovers the lost bandwidth without a silicon revision.

## ML research

### Paper reports Mixture-of-Experts models degrade faster than dense models on repeated training data

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11917v1)
- **Blurb:** A preprint reports Mixture-of-Experts models degrade at 4x data repetition where 80M dense models tolerate 8x.
- **Summary:** The preprint compares sparse and dense architectures under repeated training data and reports degradation onset at 4x repetition for Mixture-of-Experts models against 8x for 80M-parameter dense models. The results are the authors' own and are not independently reproduced.
- **Why it matters:** Teams repeating data on MoE architectures hit degradation at 4x repetition where 80M dense models tolerate 8x, which changes data budget planning for sparse training runs.

### GPU-CFR compiles counterfactual regret minimization to static dataflow and CUDA graph replay

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11923v1)
- **Blurb:** GPU-CFR compiles counterfactual regret minimization into static dataflow replayed as a single CUDA graph launch.
- **Summary:** The preprint fixes the operation sequence ahead of execution and replays it through a CUDA graph, which removes per-kernel dispatch cost from the inner loop. The reported speedups are the authors' own and are not independently reproduced.
- **Why it matters:** Fixing the operation sequence ahead of time and replaying it as one graph launch removes the per-kernel dispatch overhead that had kept this workload faster on CPUs.
