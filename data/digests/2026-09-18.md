## ML research

### Component-level harness study finds bash-only agents cost less and rule-based elision beats recoverable context

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49753878)
- **Blurb:** A 176-setting harness study finds bash-only agents cost less and rule-based elision beats LLM summarization alone.
- **Summary:** The paper fixes the execution loop and varies planning, action space and context management across 176 matched settings on SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1 with four models. It reports that staging rule-based elision before LLM summarization gives the strongest overall efficiency among the context-management strategies, that making elided content recoverable adds machinery models rarely use and gains no accuracy, that planning shifts from an accuracy scaffold on weaker models to a cost saver on stronger ones, and that bash-capable models run a bash-only interface at substantially lower cost.
- **Why it matters:** The matched-setting comparison is the one most harness write-ups skip, so the results are directly actionable for anyone choosing an action space or a context policy.

### Telstra outage traced to a GPS week rollover and an NTP timing loop

- **Category:** Outage
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.netnod.se/blog/telstra-outage-night-network-decided-year-was-2006), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49748957)
- **Blurb:** An independent review traces Telstra's 2026-07-08 outage to a GPS week rollover on one receiver and an NTP timing loop.
- **Summary:** Telstra commissioned an independent review from Technology Audit Partners, which attributes the 2026-07-08 outage to one GPS receiver that had not been patched for the GPS week rollover and so set its time to 2006, and to an NTP timing loop that carried that time into the network. - **Category:** Dev tools

- **Blurb:** Zed opened the Delta public beta and reports 33 people landing 570 changes to main with pull requests turned off.
- **Summary:** Delta replaces the pull request with a thread a teammate joins, sharing the same worktrees and the originating agent's context, and each review gets an isolated copy of the parent worktrees. It is built on DeltaDB, which records edits between commits alongside human and agent messages while leaving the commit as the checkpoint teams push and build from, so a repository stays a normal Git repository for anyone who never opens Delta. ### jemalloc 5.4.0 removes seven tcache tuning options and silently ignores their malloc_conf settings

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://github.com/jemalloc/jemalloc/releases/tag/5.4.0), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49750152)
- **Blurb:** jemalloc 5.4.0 removes seven tcache tuning options and ignores their malloc_conf settings without any error.
- **Summary:** The tcache fill and retention targets now adapt per bin to demand observed between GC events, replacing the fixed refill and flush policy, and the options lg_tcache_nslots_mul, tcache_nslots_small_min, tcache_nslots_small_max, tcache_nslots_large, tcache_gc_delay_bytes, lg_tcache_flush_small_div and lg_tcache_flush_large_div are gone. Settings for them in malloc_conf are ignored without error and the matching opt mallctls return ENOENT. The release also preserves errno across free, free_sized and free_aligned_sized, accepts NULL in free_sized and free_aligned_sized for C23 correctness, and fixes a potential deadlock in arena_reset.
- **Why it matters:** A deployment tuned against those options loses its tuning quietly on upgrade, because the allocator neither errors nor warns on the removed settings.

## Engineering posts

### Uber caps retry storms by making services claim ownership of errors

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/protecting-against-retry-storms/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49746628)
- **Blurb:** Uber cut its maximum retry storm radius from 25 hops to 3 by making each service declare which errors it owns.
- **Summary:** Uber states that retry budgets alone still let a deep call chain amplify one failure, because every hop retries against its own budget. The post describes marking which service owns an error so callers stop retrying failures they do not own, and reports the maximum retry storm radius falling from 25 hops to 3.
- **Why it matters:** The change is in what an error carries rather than in added capacity, so it applies to any deep RPC chain with per-hop retries.

### FEX-Emu measures what emulating x86 total store ordering costs on five ARM cores

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://fex-emu.com/Scourge-of-emulation/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49750094)
- **Blurb:** FEX-Emu benchmarks x86 store-ordering emulation on five ARM cores and finds a hardware TSO toggle beats LRCPC.
- **Summary:** The post benchmarks AmpereOne, Cortex-X4, Cortex-X925, Oryon-3 and Apple M1, separating acquire-load and release-store on ARMv8.0-a, the LRCPC instructions mandatory since ARMv8.3, and Apple's hardware TSO mode. Unaligned x86 accesses force FEX to patch its JIT output back to a plain load-store wrapped in a data memory barrier, which costs roughly half the throughput on Cortex-X4 and Cortex-X925 and more on Oryon-3 stores, while the M1 with TSO enabled shows aligned and unaligned within about 5 percent. The stated conclusion is that a hardware TSO toggle beats successive LRCPC extension versions.
- **Why it matters:** The measured gap sets what x86-on-ARM emulation costs per core design, and points the fix at a silicon feature rather than at the JIT.
