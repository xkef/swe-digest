### Component-level harness study finds bash-only interfaces cut cost for bash-capable models and rule-based elision beats recoverable context

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49753878)
- **Blurb:** A 176-setting harness study finds bash-only interfaces cut cost for bash-capable models, and recoverable elided context gains no accuracy.
- **Summary:** The paper fixes the execution loop and varies planning, action space and context management across 176 matched settings on SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1 with four models. It reports that staging rule-based elision before LLM summarization gives the strongest overall efficiency among the context-management strategies, that making elided content recoverable adds machinery models rarely use and gains no accuracy, that planning shifts from an accuracy scaffold on weaker models to a cost saver on stronger ones, and that predefined tools improve performance for models with weaker bash proficiency while bash-capable models run a bash-only interface at substantially lower cost, especially on command-line-centric tasks.
- **Why it matters:** The matched-setting comparison is the one most harness write-ups skip, so the results are directly actionable for anyone choosing an action space or a context policy.

### Laser fault injection sets RP2350 debug-enable bits and recovers an OTP secret after a rescue reset

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://donjon.ledger.com/blog/rp2350-secure-debug-laser-fault-injection/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49757050)
- **Blurb:** Ledger Donjon used laser fault injection to set RP2350 debug-enable bits and restore Secure debug on a locked part.
- **Summary:** Ledger Donjon located the DEBUGEN bit sites with differential photon-emission microscopy, then set PROC1 and PROC1_SECURE with laser pulses at two positions a few micrometres apart on a revision A4 part with glitch detectors at maximum sensitivity, restoring Secure debug. The permanent debug-disable flag sits in OTP with three-of-eight and majority-vote redundancy, but the memory-mapped DEBUGEN register that can override it has no documented redundancy, parity or vote, DEBUGEN_LOCK did not prevent the fault, and a pulse could set a lock bit so firmware could not restore the disabled value. Separately, an RP-AP rescue reset halts the chip in boot-ROM wait paths before firmware applies its runtime OTP page lock, which let the researchers read the full challenge secret.
- **Why it matters:** The attack needs destructive backside decapsulation, physical access and roughly $250,000 of equipment, so the result is a design lesson about the whole enforcement path rather than a remote risk to shipped parts.
- **Follow-up:** Track a Raspberry Pi response to the 2026-07-28 disclosure.

### C++26 makes trivial infinite loops well-defined after compilers optimized embedded halt loops away

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.sandordargo.com/blog/2026/09/16/cpp26-trivial-infinite-loops), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49746406)
- **Blurb:** C++26 adopts P2809R3, making a trivially empty while (true) loop defined behaviour that optimizers may no longer delete.
- **Summary:** A `while (true);` loop with no side effects was undefined behaviour before C++26 under the C++11 forward progress rules, so the optimizer could delete it, and the post shows Clang doing exactly that and falling through into a linker-adjacent function. P2809R3 defines a narrow category, a trivially empty body plus a controlling constant expression that is true, whose body becomes a call to std::this_thread::yield, and amends the forward progress guarantee to allow continuing such a loop, while on freestanding implementations whether the yield replacement happens at all is implementation-defined. The proposal was also accepted as a defect report, so implementations may apply it to earlier language modes, which is why the old behaviour may not reproduce even in C++20, and C has had the equivalent rule since C11.
- **Why it matters:** The halt-on-error loop used in embedded and kernel code with no operating system to exit to could be deleted, leaving the device running on through corrupt state.

### Uber caps retry storms by making services claim ownership of errors

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/protecting-against-retry-storms/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49746628)
- **Blurb:** Uber cut its maximum retry storm radius from 25 hops to 3 by making each service declare which errors it owns.
- **Summary:** Uber states that retry budgets alone still let a deep call chain amplify one failure, because every hop retries against its own budget. ### FEX-Emu measures what emulating x86 total store ordering costs on five ARM cores

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://fex-emu.com/Scourge-of-emulation/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49750094)
- **Blurb:** FEX-Emu benchmarks x86 store-ordering emulation on five ARM cores and finds a hardware TSO toggle beats LRCPC.
- **Summary:** The post benchmarks AmpereOne, Cortex-X4, Cortex-X925, Oryon-3 and Apple M1, separating acquire-load and release-store on ARMv8.0-a, the LRCPC instructions mandatory since ARMv8.3, and Apple's hardware TSO mode. Unaligned x86 accesses force FEX to patch its JIT output back to a plain load-store wrapped in a data memory barrier, which costs roughly half the throughput on Cortex-X4 and Cortex-X925 and more on Oryon-3 stores, while the M1 with TSO enabled shows aligned and unaligned within about 5 percent. The stated conclusion is that a hardware TSO toggle beats successive LRCPC extension versions.
- **Why it matters:** The measured gap sets what x86-on-ARM emulation costs per core design, and points the fix at a silicon feature rather than at the JIT.
