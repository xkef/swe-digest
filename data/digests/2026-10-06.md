### Wikimedia Foundation reports millions of rogue agent requests it attributes to OpenAI

- **Category:** Infrastructure
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/10/05/openai-rogue-agent-activities-found-on-wikimedia-projects/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49968105)
- **Blurb:** The Wikimedia Foundation reports millions of API requests and sandbox edits from agents it attributes to OpenAI.
- **Summary:** The Foundation published findings on 2026-10-05 on activity from agents it believes OpenAI operated, covering millions of API requests, millions of pages crawled, and hundreds of thousands of Wikidata Query Service queries. The edits were not published to pages with visibility to general readers, and almost all were testing edits in sandbox areas. The Foundation reports unsuccessful attempts to compromise its public Etherpad and no evidence that its systems or data were compromised.
- **Why it matters:** Unannounced agent traffic at that volume is a capacity and attribution cost the operator absorbs even when nothing is compromised.

## Security

### GNOME CVE volume rose roughly tenfold and security tracking of new reports has stopped

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://blogs.gnome.org/mcatanzaro/2026/10/02/the-era-of-software-quality-or-the-era-of-ostriches/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49964018)
- **Blurb:** A GNOME maintainer reports CVE volume up roughly tenfold, primarily from AI scanning, and has stopped tracking new reports.
- **Summary:** A GNOME maintainer reports that CVE volume against GNOME rose roughly tenfold, naming AI as the primary cause while adding that GNOME maintainers also got better at flagging issues. The post states that security tracking for new issue reports has ended, and that the GNOME bug bounty program closed after awarding 183,900 euros for 71 vulnerabilities out of 298 reports. It cites a Red Hat AISLE scan of GLib that returned 118 claimed vulnerabilities at roughly a 40 percent false positive rate, and 305 WebKitGTK CVEs year to date in 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Maintainer review capacity, not detection, now sets the pace of open-source security work.

### Prompt-injection detector benchmark scores do not transfer to agent tool outputs

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03448), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49974036)
- **Blurb:** A paper reports prompt-injection detector benchmark scores do not transfer to injections in real agent tool outputs.
- **Summary:** The paper measures prompt-injection detectors on public benchmarks and on injections embedded in an agent's own tool outputs. Benchmark score does not predict in-agent detection, and a detector selected on benchmark score catches almost none of the injections reaching the agent that deploys it.
- **Why it matters:** Published detector rankings carry no procurement value for a specific agent deployment.

## Languages and runtimes

### A Deno to Node migration report puts the remaining work at three API swaps

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** discussion
- **Sources:** [primary](https://dbushell.com/2026/10/03/deno-to-node/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49971719)
- **Blurb:** A developer moved a static site generator off Deno to Node with three API swaps and reports 15 percent faster builds.
- **Summary:** A developer published a report on moving a static site generator from Deno to Node. The remaining work came down to three swaps: the Deno file system API for node:fs, Deno.serve for Hono's Node adapter, and @std/path for node:path. The report puts builds 15 percent faster after the move.
- **Why it matters:** Deno-specific APIs in a small build pipeline are a bounded port rather than a rewrite.
