### Atlassian patches a pre-auth file read in eight products, six of them Data Center editions, and a public exploit chain turns it into Jira administrator

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-gr66-x5pq-8g2g), [research](https://labs.watchtowr.com/you-wont-hear-about-these-even-in-myths-atlassian-jira-confluence-and-more-pre-auth-arbitrary-file-read-cve-2026-21589/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50005577)

- **Summary:** The GitHub advisory record GHSA-gr66-x5pq-8g2g carries the Atlassian advisory text for CVE-2026-21589, rated CVSS v4 9.3 critical under CWE-552, with the base metrics `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:L/AT:N/PR:N/UI:N`, so attack complexity is low, there are no attack requirements, and no privileges or user interaction are needed. NVD published it on 2026-10-05. The record names eight products: Bitbucket, Confluence, Jira Service Management, Jira Software, Bamboo and Crowd in their Data Center editions, plus Crucible and Fisheye, which it does not label Data Center. All versions are affected, back to releases as old as Bitbucket 4.6.0 and Confluence 5.10.0. The fix list reads Bitbucket 9.4.26, 10.2.8 and 10.5.1, Confluence 9.2.26 and 10.2.19, Crowd 6.3.7, 7.0.3, 7.1.1 and 7.2.4, Jira Software 9.12.40, 10.3.26 and 11.3.12, Jira Service Management 5.12.40, 10.3.26 and 11.3.12, Bamboo 10.2.24 and 12.1.12, and Crucible and Fisheye 4.9.15. The vendor advisory page itself was not read on this run, and the two sources disagree on the Crowd fix, with watchTowr listing 7.1.7 against the 7.1.1 in the fix list above.
- **Why it matters:** A path-knowledge precondition stops being a mitigation the moment one sufficient path is published, so on an unpatched install the working assumption is that the Crowd credential is already readable rather than merely reachable.
- **Follow-up:** The fixed version for Crowd confirmed from the vendor advisory page, and any report of exploitation in the wild.

### Paper argues Lean verification of AI autoformalisation proves nothing about the prose proof, and that OpenAI's Navier-Stokes case shows it

- **Category:** Paper
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49994145)
- **Blurb:** A paper argues a passing Lean check says nothing about the prose proof it was translated from, citing OpenAI's release.
- **Summary:** The authors argue that a successful Lean check validates only the formal statement that was produced, not the natural-language argument it was translated from. They place that translation problem above the halting problem in the Solvability Complexity Index hierarchy. They use OpenAI's Navier-Stokes material as the worked case.
- **Why it matters:** Any pipeline that treats a passing Lean check as evidence that a model-written argument is correct depends on a faithful natural-language to formal translation that the paper argues is not decidable.

### incident.io traces a Postgres queue ceiling to MultiXact lock contention caused by per-item savepoints

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://incident.io/blog/how-savepoints-quietly-throttled-our-postgres-queue), [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/programming/comments/1x02iea/how_savepoints_quietly_throttled_our_postgres/)
- **Blurb:** incident.io found per-item savepoints put a MultiXact in every locked row, capping its Postgres queue throughput.
- **Summary:** incident.io ran each item of a FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED batch in its own savepoint, which placed a MultiXact in every locked row. Each skipped row then cost a MultiXact lookup behind one lightweight lock, so adding workers made claim acquisition slower. Splitting the claim from the work gave a 3.5x throughput gain.
- **Why it matters:** A queue built on SKIP LOCKED can hit a ceiling that looks like database load but comes from how the claim transaction is structured.

### Push ifs up and fors down, with the algebra that says when each rewrite is legal

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://debasishg.github.io/blog/push-ifs-up-fors-down/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49997073)
- **Blurb:** A post gives the preconditions that make pushing conditions up and loops down a legal rewrite rather than a style rule.
- **Summary:** The post states the preconditions behind the idiom. A condition only leaves a loop when it is loop invariant. A selection moves below a join only when it touches one side. Filtering before mapping holds as filter p . map f == map f . filter (p . f) and pays only when p . f reduces to a cheap test on the input.
- **Why it matters:** The idiom usually circulates as a bare style rule, and the stated preconditions are what separate a safe rewrite from a behaviour change.
