## Top stories

### Apple moves EU app distribution to one set of terms and replaces the Core Technology Fee with a 5 percent commission

- **Category:** Apple
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49348055)
- **Summary:** Apple states the changes resolve its disagreements with the European Commission over business terms and alternative distribution, and that on 2026-10-01 all EU app distribution moves to one set of business terms with the per-install Core Technology Fee replaced by a 5 percent Core Technology Commission on digital transactions in apps distributed outside the App Store. Commission rates are set at 26 percent for App Store apps using Apple In-App Purchase, 20 percent for alternative payment processing, 15 percent for link-outs, and 15 or 10 percent for Small Business, Mini Apps, and Video Partner Program developers, and a chosen payment configuration must be held for 12 months. Apple In-App Purchase may now run alongside alternative payment options, which was not previously permitted in the EU, eligibility to run an alternative marketplace or distribute via the web widens to five new qualification routes, and Notarization still applies to every alternatively distributed app. Apple also sets child safety requirements in the same announcement: Kids category apps may not link out to websites for transactions, apps using alternative payment processing or link-outs must include a parental gate for users under 18, and no app may link out for transactions for users under 13.
- **Why it matters:** Every developer distributing in the EU has a dated switch to new terms on 2026-10-01, and per-install cost exposure becomes a percentage of transaction value instead.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether the European Commission accepts the revised terms and whether the 5 percent commission is challenged.

### GitHub publishes the root cause for its 7 hour 47 minute outage: an Istio sidecar scaling gap, HAProxy flow exhaustion, and a VS Code retry storm

- **Category:** Outage
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [GitHub Status incidents API, a rolling feed](https://www.githubstatus.com/api/v2/incidents.json), [GitHub Status incident page, the durable citation](https://www.githubstatus.com/incidents/zkxwbgr0cnmx)
- **Summary:** GitHub published the root cause analysis for the 2026-08-17 outage, which ran 13:28 to 21:15 UTC with web and API error rates near 20 percent and archive and raw content near 50 percent. GitHub attributes the trigger to network saturation on Central US load balancers, originating in an Istio sidecar pod that hit its concurrency limit and failed to autoscale because the scaling policy watched host service limits and not sidecar limits, which cascaded until four HAProxy nodes exhausted their flow limits and degraded the gateway authentication path, and pausing HAProxy on those nodes produced immediate broad recovery. A latent retry bug in VS Code amplified Copilot token traffic roughly tenfold, taking the Copilot Token Service from 7 to 9 thousand requests per second to 70 to 100 thousand.
- **Why it matters:** The length of the outage came from client and gateway retry amplification rather than from the original fault, which is the transferable lesson for anyone running retrying clients behind a shared gateway.
- **Follow-up:** This closes the analysis GitHub promised on 2026-08-17. Track the further root cause analysis GitHub promised for the 2026-08-18 runner-group incident.

### Modular open sources the Mojo compiler and toolchain under Apache 2.0 with LLVM exceptions

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [Modular blog](https://www.modular.com/blog/mojo-open-source), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49348079)
- **Summary:** Modular states the Mojo compiler, tooling, and everything needed to build the language are now in the modular GitHub repository under Apache 2.0 with LLVM exceptions, one week after Mojo reached 1.0 with source stability. - **Why it matters:** Four years of closed compiler work becomes inspectable and forkable, while the development process itself stays closed until Modular opens contributions.

## Agentic coding

### Linear reports coding-agent teams roughly tripled weekly pull requests over two years

- **Category:** Agentic coding
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [Linear data page](https://linear.app/data), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49353432)
- **Summary:** Across a fixed cohort of 6,887 paid teams, 4,280 with a connected coding agent and 2,607 without, Linear reports agent teams went from 21 to 65 pull requests opened per week between June 2024 and June 2026 while teams without one went from 8 to 10. Pull requests per paid workspace are up 111 percent against a June 2024 baseline, with the inflection through 2026, and issues authored by agents and MCP clients went from under one in a thousand two years ago to near parity with people and integrations in the week of 2026-08-03, at 2,435 thousand against 2,481 thousand per week. Linear states its own limits: it cannot see AI usage outside Linear, it counts pull requests opened rather than merged, an opened pull request says nothing about the value of the change, and agent-connected teams were already higher output before coding agents existed.
- **Why it matters:** This is measured cohort data with stated sample sizes and stated caveats rather than a vendor productivity claim, and the comparison cohort shows little change over the same period.

### Vercel Labs publishes fx, a Zig coding agent that embeds through WebAssembly and ACP

- **Category:** Agentic coding
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [fx repository](https://github.com/vercel-labs/fx), [fx.sh](https://fx.sh), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49353339)
- **Summary:** fx is a coding agent harness and CLI written in Zig under Apache 2.0, model agnostic, aimed at research and embedding inside larger systems rather than at being a terminal IDE, with the repository marking the status experimental at 284 stars and a 7.8 MiB binary. It builds as a native binary or WebAssembly, and hosts can supply network transport, session storage, configuration, permission handling, and terminal I/O through `createFxAgent` with `fx-core.wasm`, `createFxTerminal` with `fx-term.wasm`, or `fx acp` for Agent Client Protocol clients. It ships skills, MCP, subagents, and a permissions model with stored rules addressable by stable rule IDs, and building from source needs Zig 0.16.0 or later.
- **Why it matters:** Compiling an agent loop to WebAssembly puts it inside a host process rather than behind a subprocess, and ACP support makes it addressable from existing clients without a bespoke integration.

## Security

### Microsoft patches CoSnitch, a one-click Copilot exfiltration chain that Copilot itself disclosed

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [Varonis Threat Labs](https://www.varonis.com/blog/cosnitch), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/08/microsoft-copilot-reveals-secret-input-that-allowed-it-to-be-hacked/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49351878), [second HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49351290)
- **Summary:** Varonis Threat Labs reports CVE-2026-24301, rated critical, disclosed to Microsoft in December 2025 with patches shipped 2026-08-18, and reports no evidence of exploitation in the wild. Three flaws chain: an undocumented URL parameter, `autorun=1`, that fires an attacker prompt supplied in `?q=` on page load with no user gesture, exfiltration of connected Gmail, Drive, Calendar, chat history, and memory contents through Copilot's own URL fetch capability with the payload base64 encoded into the path, and indirect prompt injection via web summarization that writes attacker instructions into the persistent memory store. Varonis states the memory writes survive password changes, session revocation, and device re-enrollment, and that the researchers found the parameter by repeatedly asking Copilot why auto-execution was impossible until it named the parameter and its disabled protections, a technique they call meta-hacking. Copilot is a hosted service, so no client version scope applies and no affected version range is published: the fix is server side and shipped 2026-08-18. The two sources disagree on the affected product. Varonis, the primary source, describes Microsoft Copilot Personal at copilot.microsoft.com and dates disclosure to December 2025, while the Ars account describes Microsoft 365 Copilot for enterprise and states Microsoft silently mitigated the `?q=` injection in February before shipping fuller fixes. This block follows Varonis, and the enterprise framing is not asserted here.
- **Why it matters:** The exfiltration traffic is an ordinary outbound HTTPS GET from Copilot, so network-layer tooling sees nothing anomalous, and injected instructions held in memory persist through the account recovery steps a defender would normally take.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether Microsoft publishes an advisory for CVE-2026-24301 naming the affected product, which would settle whether the enterprise framing holds.

### turbovec publishes a Rust TurboQuant vector index with reproducible benchmarks against FAISS

- **Category:** Infrastructure
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [turbovec repository](https://github.com/RyanCodrai/turbovec), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49349898)
- **Summary:** turbovec is an MIT-licensed Rust vector index with Python bindings built on Google Research's TurboQuant, a data-oblivious quantizer that needs no separate training phase, and it is a third-party implementation rather than a Google project despite the Hacker News title. Every published number states corpus, query count, k, run count, and the exact two machines, with the benchmark scripts committed and per-cell result JSON linked. Against FAISS IndexPQFastScan it reports averages of about 3.4 to 3.5 times at 4-bit and 20 to 26 percent at 2-bit on both ARM and x86, and it names the cells where plain TurboQuant trails FAISS rather than reporting only wins. The repository shows 15.3 thousand stars.
- **Why it matters:** Online ingest with no train step, incremental crash-safe saves, and allowlist filtering applied inside the SIMD kernel are the operationally useful parts, and the stated measurement conditions make the comparison checkable rather than a launch claim.

## Engineering posts

### A failed Framework BIOS update leaves no recovery path short of external SPI flashing

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [quantum5.ca write-up, 2026-08-16](https://quantum5.ca/2026/08/16/fixing-bricked-amd-7040-series-framework-13-laptop-with-20-tools/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49345220)
- **Summary:** The author's BIOS 3.20 update on a Framework 13 with AMD Ryzen 7040, applied through fwupd after a Framework newsletter recommended it, hung mid-flash and never recovered, and Framework support offered only a motherboard purchase at CA$500 or more because the one-year warranty had expired. The write-up documents recovering it instead by reprogramming the Winbond W25Q256JW SPI chip in circuit with roughly US$20 of pogo-pin tooling, and covers the chip datasheet, the 1.8 volt requirement, the 32 MiB image size, and why the CH341A programmer is a poor choice at about 1.7 MHz. The durable finding is architectural: the WSON package has nothing to clamp, the board has an unpopulated socket footprint and an unpopulated flash header, and there is no BIOS recovery mechanism, which the author contrasts with Dell BIOS recovery, HP Sure Start, and a 2004 ASUS board.
- **Why it matters:** A firmware update path with no recovery mechanism turns one failed flash into a mainboard replacement, on a machine sold on repairability.
- **Follow-up:** Claims about other affected users rest on Framework forum threads the author cites, not on a Framework statement. Track whether Framework adds a recovery mechanism.
