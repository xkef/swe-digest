### OpenAI reports a Navier-Stokes resolution, and Buckmaster disputes how it got there

- **Category:** AI
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [OpenAI account, relayed](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/), [Buckmaster statement](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/openai-fought-dirty-on-career-making-math-problem-says-nyu-mathematician/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605915), [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49624163)
- **Blurb:** OpenAI reports an unreleased model resolved Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness, and Tristan Buckmaster disputes the credit.
- **Summary:** OpenAI states an unreleased internal model produced a resolution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, with the agents reaching it on 2026-09-05 about 88 hours after launch and Lean formalization and verification taking a further 17 hours through GPT-6 Astra, using 2.7 million agent messages and approximately 130 billion output tokens on that problem within 4.9 million messages and about 300 billion output tokens across all attempted problems. Tristan Buckmaster published a statement alleging OpenAI took up the approach he developed with his collaborator Levent Alpoge, an Anthropic employee whom OpenAI stated it would not invite as a co-author because of its competitive relationship with his employer, after learning of it from him. OpenAI states that neither its researchers nor its agents saw any of that work before public release and that no specific user data was accessed to solve the problem, states separately that it cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from Buckmaster and Alpoge's product usage helped improve its models, and records that its proofs differ significantly from theirs with the precise results proved differing in the Euler case.
- **Why it matters:** A vendor's agents are credited with a Millennium Prize resolution that has not been peer reviewed, and the contested question is whether original work run through that vendor's coding agent stays the user's own.
- **Follow-up:** Track peer review of the resolution, OpenAI's further response to the statement, and any correction to either account.

### WeWorm: a zero-click worm that spreads through WeChat calls across iOS and Android

- **Category:** Security
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://calif.io/research/weworm), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49609900)
- **Blurb:** Calif.io details WeWorm, a zero-click WeChat VoIP worm that takes over accounts of a caller's contacts on iOS and Android.
- **Summary:** Calif.io describes a memory-corruption bug in the VoIP stack of WeChat that yields full account takeover through an incoming call, with no user interaction, on both iOS and Android, and states the exploit requires the attacker to be on the victim's friend list. Calif.io notes the barrier is low because each compromised account reaches the contacts of the next victim, and that declining the call stops that attempt while the attacker can retry. Calif.io reported the bug to Tencent on 2026-07-24, Tencent published WeChat Android 8.0.77 and iOS 8.0.76 on 2026-08-21, and Calif.io states server-side mitigation was confirmed for all users on 2026-08-28.
- **Why it matters:** The friend-list precondition bounds each hop rather than the spread, and the team reports two days from bug to first remote code execution exploit working with AI, plus one further week to build the worm.
- **Follow-up:** Track any confirmation of exploitation in the wild.

### Read the Docs details a ten-day DDoS that peaked at 5.5 million requests per minute

- **Category:** Outage
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://about.readthedocs.com/blog/2026/09/2026-ddos-attack/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49628614)
- **Blurb:** Read the Docs details a ten-day DDoS that peaked at 5.5 million requests per minute from millions of IP addresses.
- **Summary:** Read the Docs reports that the June 2026 attack peaked at 5.5 million requests per minute against a normal daily peak under 100k, ran for nearly ten days, and came from millions of IP addresses across hundreds of ASNs. The attackers randomized HTTP headers and TLS parameters, so JA3 and JA4 fingerprinting did not help, and they targeted cache misses such as 404s and uncached 302 redirects in a yo-yo pattern timed to rate-limit windows. The team credits a penalty box that rate limits on the ratio of expensive uncached responses per fingerprint, ASN or domain, with edge and WAF rules managed in Terraform.
- **Why it matters:** The mitigation keys on the cost of the response rather than on client identity, which is the part that survives randomized fingerprints.

### Replacing a 16-byte Rust enum with a 64-bit tagged word made every interpreter benchmark faster

- **Category:** Languages
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://pointersgonewild.com/2026-08-25-replacing-a-rust-enum-with-a-64-bit-word/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49575914)
- **Blurb:** Replacing a 16-byte Rust enum with a 64-bit tagged word cut peak RSS by up to 37 percent and sped every benchmark.
- **Summary:** Rust's alignment rules pushed a tagged enum whose largest variant needs 64 bits out to a full 128 bits. Replacing it with low-bit tagging in a single 64-bit word cut peak RSS by up to 37 percent and improved every benchmark, including the float-heavy ones the author expected to regress.
- **Why it matters:** Enum layout in Rust can silently double a value representation, and the cost shows up as memory traffic rather than as anything visible in the source.

### A 40-year-old off-by-one in XNU gives the first UNIX-domain socket a changing inode

- **Category:** Apple
- **Status:** confirmed

### A looped-transformer explainer weighs the reported recurrent depth in GPT-6 Astra

- **Category:** Engineering post
- **Status:** discussion
- **Sources:** [primary](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49627370)
- **Blurb:** Sebastian Raschka explains looped transformers on Nanbeige4.2-3B, which runs 22 shared blocks twice for depth 44.
- **Summary:** Raschka explains looped transformers using the open-weight Nanbeige4.2-3B, which applies one stack of 22 blocks twice for 44 block applications with weights shared across passes. He sets out the tradeoff: effective depth doubles and transformer-block parameter memory roughly halves against 44 distinct blocks, while the forward pass and backpropagation still run through all 44 applications, so compute is not saved. He frames the claim that Astra uses recurrent depth as reporting by The Information rather than as a confirmed architecture, and treats the hidden chain-of-thought inference drawn from it as the part that does not follow.
- **Why it matters:** Weight sharing trades parameter memory for compute, so a looped model is cheaper to hold and no cheaper to run.
