### Haiku R1/beta6 ships after two years with a new userland malloc and halved WebKit build time

- **Category:** Dev tools
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [release notes](https://www.haiku-os.org/get-haiku/r1beta6/release-notes/), [announcement](https://www.haiku-os.org/news/2026-08-26_haiku_r1_beta6), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49499867)
- **Blurb:** Haiku R1/beta6 ships after two years, cutting a full HaikuWebKit rebuild from 4h53m to 2h33m and replacing its malloc.
- **Summary:** The release notes record over 530 tickets resolved since beta5, a full HaikuWebKit rebuild falling from 4h53m to 2h33m, and `git status` in a 160,000-file repository falling from about 33s to 20s cold and from 15s to 2.5s hot. The named causes are a directory-entry cache that no longer takes a write lock to add entries, read-write locking in the inode block cache, and a new userland malloc based on OpenBSD's with a global caching layer, replacing an aging hoard fork. The release also adds NVMM hardware virtualization for QEMU on x86_64 through `-accel nvmm`, officially branded Firefox after Mozilla granted permission, and a Go 1.26.1 port, and memory work brings 32-bit boot back within 128 MB of RAM.
- **Why it matters:** The performance claims are stated as measured before-and-after numbers with named causes in the kernel's caching and locking paths, which is the form that can be checked rather than taken on trust.

### Asahi Linux posts initial USB4 and Thunderbolt support for Apple M1, M2 and M3 to the kernel list

- **Category:** Linux/Kernel
- **Status:** developing
- **Sources:** [patch series](https://lore.kernel.org/asahi/20260830-b4-apple-soc-tbt-v1-0-44bc9348683c@kernel.org/), [author post](https://social.treehouse.systems/@sven/117186314116319619), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49502515)
- **Blurb:** Sven Peter posted a 19-patch series adding initial USB4 and Thunderbolt support for Apple M1, M2 and M3 to the kernel list.
- **Summary:** The series, posted on 2026-08-30, adds 3872 lines including a 1028-line `drivers/thunderbolt/apple.c`. Each USB4-capable Type-C port carries an Apple Converged I/O block with a Cortex-M3 co-processor, and the main SoC bus sees only a 16 MiB window into its MMIO space while that co-processor runs, so the NHI and the DART IOMMU are modelled as device-tree children populated after boot and removed before power-down. The stated scope is bounded: only XDomain connections and USB3-over-USB4 tunnels work, PCIe and DisplayPort tunneling need reverse engineering that is not done, and suspend is blocked whenever a connection is active because resume hits an SError.
- **Why it matters:** Apple Silicon laptops running Linux gain a path to their USB4 ports, though the series is posted for review, depends on five other in-flight series, and lands nothing today.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether the series is taken through the thunderbolt tree and whether PCIe and DisplayPort tunneling follow.

### Sander Dieleman surveys the return of continuous diffusion language models

- **Category:** ML research
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://sander.ai/2026/08/24/continuous-dlms.html), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49502611)
- **Blurb:** Sander Dieleman traces why continuous diffusion for language died after 2023 and what changed to bring it back.
- **Summary:** The concrete anchor is Gulrajani and Hashimoto's May 2023 measurement that a likelihood-based continuous diffusion model was 64x less training-efficient than an autoregressive baseline, which Dieleman argues was disqualifying while the field was still optimising Chinchilla-style compute against perplexity. The technical section covers embedding strategies from one-hot through pre-trained to jointly learned and the collapse failure mode of the last, the pairing of loss function to unembedding strategy, and why noise schedules matter more here because meaningful corruption of high-dimensional embeddings happens across a narrow band of noise levels. He flags the account as subjective and the causal claims as speculative.
- **Why it matters:** A DeepMind research scientist writing on his own domain sets out the design choices and failure modes anyone building a continuous diffusion language model has to make first.

### omniflake puts about twelve thousand Nix flakes behind one input, and its author submits a quadratic lock-naming fix upstream

- **Category:** Dev tools
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Sources:** [primary](https://fzakaria.com/2026/08/28/one-flake-to-rule-them-all), [project](https://omniflake.com/), [discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49497712)
- **Blurb:** omniflake exposes about twelve thousand Nix flakes behind one input, and its author submitted a lock-naming fix to Nix.
- **Summary:** Declaring thousands of flakes as inputs failed, because Nix evaluates every input to write the lock file and one unlockable input aborts the run. It was also quadratic: every flake brings its own `systems` input, so 4000 inputs produced nodes named `systems_2` through `systems_4000` with the collision search restarting at `_2` each time, roughly 8 million string formats per evaluation. Zakaria's fix, filed as NixOS/nix#16387, remembers the highest suffix per name and resumes there, reports about 21x faster locking at 4000 inputs, and writes byte-identical lock files, while omniflake itself abandons inputs entirely, storing a JSONL table of locked pins and fetching each flake on demand with `builtins.fetchTree` at a cost of about 1.5s for `nix flake lock` and six inherited nodes.
- **Why it matters:** The quadratic lock-naming path affects any flake with a large input set, independent of whether anyone adopts omniflake.
- **Follow-up:** Track whether NixOS/nix#16387 is merged and which Nix release carries it.
